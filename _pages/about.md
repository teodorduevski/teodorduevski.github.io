## Working Papers

### [Human Capital Breadth and the Financing of Innovative Startups](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5239857)

**Awards:** WFA–Brattle Group Ph.D. Candidate Award for Outstanding Research (2026); ENTFIN Best Doctoral Student Paper Award (2025).

<details style="margin: 0.4rem 0 1.3rem 0;">
  <summary style="cursor: pointer;">Show abstract</summary>
  <p>I examine how the breadth of venture capital (VC) partners' human capital influences investment selection, startup performance, and innovation. Partners with broader human capital are more likely to lead investments in novel startups with previously unexplored business models and significantly increase their likelihood of major success; however, they underperform when leading non-novel deals. Exploiting plausibly exogenous variation in partner time constraints as a shock to the within-VC firm likelihood of leading a deal, I provide causal evidence for these effects. A theoretical model endogenizes startup creation, partner assignment, and investment to rationalize the empirical findings and provide additional testable predictions. The results highlight the nuanced value of human capital breadth in financing innovation.</p>
</details>

### [Can Limited Partners Mitigate Negative Externalities in Private Equity?](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4641071)

*With Tianhao Yao and Chhavi Rastogi*

**Media coverage:** [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2cll8d60ulbur92i2gmio/corner-office/esg-scandals-hurt-a-pe-firms-ability-to-fundraise-but-not) · [Coller Capital Private Equity Findings](https://www.collercapital.com/private-capital-findings-issue-21/pc-findings-21-time-to-engage/) · [New Private Markets](https://www.newprivatemarkets.com/practitioners-and-academics-must-collaborate-to-bolster-private-markets-sustainability/) · [Option Finance](https://www.optionfinance.fr/analyses/la-reglementation-esg-change-lallocation-des-fonds-mais-non-le-comportement-des-entreprises-polluantes.html)

<details style="margin: 0.4rem 0 1.3rem 0;">
  <summary style="cursor: pointer;">Show abstract</summary>
  <p>We show how Limited Partners' (LPs ) environmental and social (E&amp;S) concerns transmit to private equity (PE) firms through capital supply. E&amp;S incidents in portfolio companies reduce PE fundraising, as E&amp;S-concerned relationship LPs refrain from recommitting and are not easily substituted. Using a legal reform that expands E&amp;S-concerned public pension capital to PE, we causally show that PE firms internalise LPs' E&amp;S concerns, reducing dirty sector portfolio allocation and increasing ESG hiring. Additionally, PE firms with E&amp;S-concerned relationship LPs engage with portfolio companies to manage E&amp;S risks. Limited capital substitutability enables LPs to delegate E&amp;S preferences, shaping PE allocation and engagement.</p>
</details>

### [Capital Allocation Concentration Measurement in Venture Capital](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5216676)

*With Viyacheslav Bazaliy*

<details style="margin: 0.4rem 0 1.3rem 0;">
  <summary style="cursor: pointer;">Show abstract</summary>
  <p>We revisit the measurement of capital allocation concentration in the venture capital (VC) industry and highlight the shortcomings of the widely used Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI). We show that HHI-based concentration measures are sensitive to discrepancies in VC database coverage, industry taxonomies, and classification granularity, yielding divergent trends even when applied to identical VC portfolios. To overcome these limitations, we develop a novel concentration metric using large language model (LLM) text embeddings that capture the semantic similarity among financed startups beyond predefined industry classifications. Using matched PitchBook and Crunchbase data, we validate our approach and show that OpenAI embeddings outperform alternative models on signal-to-noise and retrieval tasks. Applying this methodology, we document that aggregate VC capital allocation concentration has increased more sharply than suggested by HHI measures. A novel decomposition shows that 40% of the growth in capital allocation concentration stems from an increase in within-sector similarity among founded startups, an effect that industry-based measures do not capture.</p>
</details>

## Work in Progress

### Exploration, Exploitation and Agency Issues in Venture Capital

*Draft available upon request.*

<details style="margin: 0.4rem 0 1.3rem 0;">
  <summary style="cursor: pointer;">Show abstract</summary>
  <p>This paper examines how venture capital (VC) firms allocate capital between known markets (exploitation) and new markets (exploration). I develop a dynamic agency model with moral hazard and learning, where a VC raises funds from a limited partner (LP) and chooses its portfolio strategy. In equilibrium, firms with high opportunity costs avoid exploration, while those that explore base their decisions on fund management costs and learning ability. LPs' threat of divestment helps mitigate moral hazard and can encourage exploration. Using PitchBook data, I construct a novel measure of the opportunity cost of exploration based on VC partners' prior investment experience. Empirically, firms with higher exploration costs specialize more, perform better in their first fund, but exhibit slower growth in subsequent fundraising.</p>
</details>

### Product Market Competition and Intangible Investment

*Draft available upon request.*

<details style="margin: 0.4rem 0 1.3rem 0;">
  <summary style="cursor: pointer;">Show abstract</summary>
  <p>I study how product market competition shapes firms' intangible investment decisions. I extend the seminal d'Aspremont and Jacquemin (1988) framework to incorporate two forms of intangible investments: production-based (fully non-rival, excludable) and demand-shifter (partially non-rival, partially excludable) in a multi-product market setting. The model predicts that production-based investments increase with a firm's product-market span, while investment in demand-shifting intangibles follows an inverted-U pattern due to cross-market spillovers. Using a panel of U.S. public firms, I find empirical evidence consistent with these predictions. These findings highlight the importance of non-rivalry, limited excludability, and spillovers in shaping intangible investment decisions in multi-market environments.</p>
</details>

## Other Papers and Pre-Ph.D. Publications

### [Nonstandard Errors](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13337)

*Journal of Finance*, 2024. Research team member, with Albert Menkveld et al.

<details style="margin: 0.4rem 0 1.3rem 0;">
  <summary style="cursor: pointer;">Show summary</summary>
  <p>Analyses of the same dataset can yield different estimates, even when researchers investigate identical hypotheses. This study measures the additional uncertainty arising from researchers' analytical decisions by comparing the results of 164 independent teams. It documents substantial variation beyond conventional sampling uncertainty and examines how reproducibility, research quality, and peer review affect that variation.</p>
</details>

### [Understanding Detrimental and Beneficial Grain Boundary Effects in Halide Perovskites](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.201804792)

*Advanced Materials*, 30(52), 1804792 (2018). With Sarah Brittman et al.

<details style="margin: 0.4rem 0 1.3rem 0;">
  <summary style="cursor: pointer;">Show summary</summary>
  <p>The study uses sensitive electron backscatter diffraction to identify grain boundaries in halide perovskite thin films and examines how they relate to charge-carrier transport and photoluminescence. It finds that some grain boundaries contribute substantial recombination losses, whereas other, amorphous boundaries exhibit more favorable optical properties. These differences point to opportunities for improving optoelectronic devices through grain-boundary engineering.</p>
</details>
